01Pipes

A | B

Every command is its own process; the pipe carries the output of A to the input of B.

grep ALARM logs/vitals.log | cut -d' ' -f2 | sort -u

cut -d' ' -f2: second column · sort -u: sort and drop duplicates.

mkfifo

A named pipe: a pipe with a name, usable by any two processes. ls -l shows a p.

mkfifo ~/ipc/alarms echo 'bed-07 ALARM' > ~/ipc/alarms & writer in the background cat ~/ipc/alarms reader

A writer waits until a reader opens the pipe. Without & the shell waits forever.

02Sockets

Two-way connection: the client sends a request, the server answers. Local: a Unix domain socket file; across machines: a network address and port.

echo "bed-12 spo2=86 ALARM" | socat - UNIX-CONNECT:ipc/station.sock netstat -lx who is listening (Unix sockets)

03Shared data, locks, semaphores

Read, change, write: two processes that both read the same old value lose an update (race condition).

admit ward-a 10 & admit ward-b 10 & at the same time wait wait for all background jobs cat ~/ward/admitted less than 20!

Lock (mutual exclusion): only one at a time in the critical section. flock FILE is the Linux tool. A lock only helps if every process uses it.

Semaphore S = number of free units

P(S): S > 0 → S − 1 and continue, otherwise wait.
V(S): someone waits → hand the unit over (S stays), otherwise S + 1.
S = 1 works like a lock, S = N lets up to N in. S never goes below 0.

04Deadlock

Each job holds one lock and waits for the other: nobody ever continues. The jobs still exist, they just make no progress.

ConditionMeaning
mutual exclusiona resource is used by one at a time
hold and waithold one resource, wait for another
no preemptionnobody takes a resource away
circular waita circle of waiting jobs

All four needed, break one. Easiest: every job takes the locks in the same order.

killall syncjob stop the stuck jobs politely

06Scheduling in Linux

Fair shares by weight. nice −20 … 19: higher = nicer = smaller share. Users may only make processes nicer.

crunch a 3 & crunch b 3 & wait two jobs share one core nice -n 10 COMMAND start with nice 10 renice -n 19 -p PID change a running process

Share = weight ÷ sum of all weights. Weights: nice 0 = 1024, 5 = 335, 10 = 110, 19 = 15. Example: 335 ÷ (1024 + 335) ≈ 24.7 %.
